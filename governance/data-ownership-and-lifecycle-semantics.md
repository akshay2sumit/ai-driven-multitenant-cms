# Data Ownership and Lifecycle Semantics

## Purpose & Authority

This document defines binding rules for data ownership, lifecycle stages, immutability, retention, and deletion. This document constrains schemas, storage, APIs, operations, and AI behavior. These rules are constitutional and cannot be overridden by implementation or convenience.

## Data Ownership Taxonomy

### Tenant-Owned Data
Tenant-Owned Data is data created by or on behalf of a tenant as part of their normal use of the system. This includes content, content versions, tenant-specific configuration, user accounts within the tenant, and tenant metadata. Ownership is exclusive to the tenant. Data is governed by tenant context and system governance. Data is never shared across tenants by default. Tenant controls lifecycle within governance limits. Deletion and retention must respect tenant intent and legal constraints.

### System-Owned Data
System-Owned Data is data required for the operation, integrity, and governance of the platform itself. This includes governance rules and records, phase logs and audit metadata, platform configuration, and system health and integrity data. Ownership belongs to the system, not tenants. Data cannot be deleted by tenants. Data is retained per governance and compliance requirements. Data exists to protect system correctness and auditability. Data supersedes tenant preferences when required.

### Derived Data
Derived Data is data generated by the system from other data. This includes rendered outputs, aggregations, indexes, and caches. Ownership follows the source data. Data has no independent authority or permanence. Data can be regenerated. Deletion of source data invalidates derived data.

### Transient / Ephemeral Data
Transient / Ephemeral Data is short-lived data that exists only to support in-process operations. This includes session data, temporary computations, and request-scoped state. Ownership belongs to the system. Data has no persistence guarantees. Data must not be relied upon for correctness. Data is automatically discarded.

### Audit & Governance Data
Audit & Governance Data is data created to ensure traceability, compliance, and accountability. This includes audit logs, change histories, governance decisions, and authorization records. Ownership belongs to the system. Data is immutable once written. Data is retained per governance policy. Data cannot be altered or deleted to "clean history." Data is required for legal and operational trust.

### AI-Related Data
AI-Related Data is data used by or produced through AI-assisted processes. This includes AI prompts and suggestions, AI-generated drafts, and AI decision traces. Ownership follows source context (tenant or system). AI does not own data. No implicit reuse or training occurs without authorization. Data has strong privacy and compliance constraints. Explicit governance is required for reuse.

## Data Lifecycle Stages

### Creation
Creation is the moment data first comes into existence in the system. Creation always occurs within a governed context. Data is immediately classified into an ownership category. Unclassified data is invalid. No "anonymous" or ownerless data can exist.

### Mutation
Mutation is any authorized change to existing data. Only mutable data may be mutated. Mutation requires explicit authorization. All mutations are auditable. History is preserved; silent overwrites are forbidden.

### Publication / Activation
Publication / Activation is when data becomes active, visible, or usable outside its authoring context. Publication is a state transition, not duplication. Publication never transfers ownership. Publication never grants mutation rights. Runtime access is controlled and read-only.

### Retention
Retention is the period during which data must be preserved. Retention policies are governance-defined. Retention applies even if a tenant requests deletion. Retention duration may differ by data category. Compliance overrides convenience.

### Archival
Archival is when data is no longer active but is preserved for reference, compliance, or audit. Archived data is read-only. Archived data is excluded from normal operations. Archival does not imply deletion. History is preserved without operational risk.

### Deletion
Deletion is the controlled removal of data from active systems. Deletion is explicit and governed. Deletion respects retention and legal holds. Deletion semantics differ by data category. Deletion is a process, not a single action.

### Expiry (Automatic)
Expiry is system-triggered lifecycle transition based on policy or time. Expiry applies only to eligible data categories. Expiry must be deterministic and auditable. Expiry never bypasses governance constraints. Automation does not bypass accountability.

## Data Immutability Rules

### Immutable by Definition
The following data is immutable once written: audit & governance records, phase history and decisions, authorization and access logs, compliance-relevant events. No updates, edits, or overwrites are permitted. Corrections are recorded as new entries, never mutations. Immutability is permanent. Trust, traceability, and legal defensibility require this immutability.

### Conditionally Mutable Data
The following data may be mutable under governance: tenant-owned content (pre-publication), configuration (via governed change), user profile data (within tenant context). Mutations require explicit authorization. All changes are versioned and auditable. Mutation never destroys history. Operational flexibility exists without loss of accountability.

### Effectively Immutable States
Some data becomes effectively immutable after a transition: published content (runtime-visible), archived records, closed phase records. No mutation is allowed after transition. Changes require creation of a new version/state. Rollbacks are not overwrites. Runtime safety and historical truth are protected.

## Deletion & Retention Semantics

### What Deletion Is
Deletion is a governed lifecycle transition, not a destructive shortcut. Deletion may mean removal from active operations, removal from user access, marking as deleted while retaining audit trace, or eventual physical removal only when legally allowed.

### What Deletion Is Not
Deletion does not mean erasing history or audit logs, bypassing retention policies, immediate physical destruction by default, or retroactively changing the past.

### Soft vs Hard Deletion (Conceptual)
Soft Deletion means data is no longer active, metadata and audit trail remain, and deletion is reversible only if governance allows. Hard Deletion means physical removal of data and is allowed only after retention period ends, no legal or compliance holds exist, and governance authorization is obtained.

### Tenant Deletion Requests
Tenant requests do not override retention or law. System enforces minimum retention. Governance decides final deletion timing. "Right to delete" is respected within legal bounds, not absolutely.

### Derived and Transient Data Handling
Derived data is deleted when source data is deleted. Transient data is automatically discarded. No retention guarantees exist for transient data.

## STOP Conditions

Immediate STOP is required if any attempt is made to mutate immutable data, if any deletion bypasses retention or audit, if any ambiguity exists in deletion or ownership, or if any unauthorized physical destruction is attempted.
