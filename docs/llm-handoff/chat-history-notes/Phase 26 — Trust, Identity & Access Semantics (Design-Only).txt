PHASE 26 — STEP 1
Identity & Actor Taxonomy (Who Can Exist and Act)

Purpose:
Define who or what can act within the system, in what capacity, and with which inherent limits—independent of authentication mechanisms or schemas.
This establishes the actor universe and prevents ambiguous or implicit authority.

🧭 ACTOR CATEGORIES (AUTHORITATIVE)
1️⃣ Human User

Definition:
A Human User is a real person acting within a tenant context.

Inherent Properties:

Always associated with exactly one tenant

Acts only through assigned roles and permissions

Subject to governance and audit

Authority Limits:

No inherent authority by existence

Authority is granted, not assumed

2️⃣ Tenant Administrator (Role-Bound Actor)

Definition:
A Tenant Administrator is a Human User acting under a privileged role within a tenant.

Inherent Properties:

Elevated permissions within the tenant boundary

No authority outside the tenant

Authority Limits:

Cannot bypass governance

Cannot alter system-owned or cross-tenant state

3️⃣ System Actor

Definition:
A System Actor represents the platform itself performing governed operations.

Inherent Properties:

Acts only under explicit governance rules

Performs maintenance, enforcement, and automation

Authority Limits:

Cannot violate invariants

Cannot act outside defined lifecycle or trust boundaries

4️⃣ Service Identity

Definition:
A Service Identity is a non-human actor representing an internal or external service.

Inherent Properties:

Scoped to a specific purpose

Operates under least-privilege principles

Authority Limits:

No human-equivalent privileges

No implicit tenant-wide access

5️⃣ AI-Assisted Actor (Non-Autonomous)

Definition:
An AI-Assisted Actor is an AI system providing suggestions or drafts under human supervision.

Inherent Properties:

Cannot initiate actions independently

Requires explicit human approval for any effect

Authority Limits:

No decision-making authority

No self-authorization or delegation

6️⃣ Governance Authority

Definition:
The Governance Authority is the constitutional authority that defines, approves, and enforces rules.

Inherent Properties:

Exists outside normal tenant roles

Controls phases, scope, and constraints

Authority Limits:

Bound by its own documented rules

Acts transparently and auditable

🔐 GLOBAL ACTOR RULES (INVARIANTS)

Every actor is explicitly classified

No anonymous or implicit actors exist

Authority is always derived, never assumed

Actor scope is enforced by boundaries

Ambiguous actor identity → STOP

PHASE 26 — STEP 2
Trust Boundaries & Authority (Where Trust Begins and Ends)

Purpose:
Define what “trust” means, where it starts and stops, and how authority is established, limited, and revoked—independent of authentication mechanisms, schemas, or code.

Trust boundaries exist to protect invariants. Authority exists to act within those boundaries—never beyond them.

🧱 TRUST BOUNDARIES (AUTHORITATIVE)
1️⃣ Tenant Boundary

Meaning:
The hard separation between one tenant and all others.

Trust Rule:

No actor trusted in one tenant is trusted in another by default.

Authority Limit:

Authority never crosses tenants without explicit governance action.

2️⃣ Authoring vs Runtime Boundary

Meaning:
The separation between mutation and delivery.

Trust Rule:

Runtime is never trusted to mutate state.

Authority Limit:

Runtime actors have read-only authority.

3️⃣ Governance Boundary

Meaning:
The separation between governed rules and execution.

Trust Rule:

Execution is never trusted to redefine rules.

Authority Limit:

Only governance may authorize phases, scope, or exceptions.

4️⃣ Human vs System Boundary

Meaning:
The separation between human intent and automated execution.

Trust Rule:

System actors execute rules; they do not invent intent.

Authority Limit:

System actors cannot exceed encoded governance.

5️⃣ AI Boundary

Meaning:
The separation between assistance and authority.

Trust Rule:

AI is never trusted as an authority.

Authority Limit:

AI cannot approve, deploy, or override decisions.

6️⃣ External Boundary

Meaning:
The separation between the platform and external systems/services.

Trust Rule:

External systems are untrusted by default.

Authority Limit:

External access is least-privilege and explicitly scoped.

🧭 AUTHORITY SOURCES (AUTHORITATIVE)
A. Governance Authority

Root source of authority

Defines and enforces boundaries and invariants

Authorizes phases and scope

B. Tenant Authority

Scoped authority within a tenant

Cannot override governance

Delegated via roles and permissions

C. System Authority

Operational authority to enforce rules

No discretionary power

D. Delegated Authority

Temporarily granted authority

Explicitly scoped and revocable

Never transitive by default

🔐 AUTHORITY RULES (INVARIANTS)

Authority is explicitly granted

Authority is scoped and revocable

No authority exists outside a trust boundary

Authority cannot override invariants

Ambiguous authority → STOP

PHASE 26 — STEP 3
Roles, Permissions & Delegation Semantics (Declarative, Non-Technical)

Purpose:
Define how authority is expressed and controlled—through roles, permissions, and delegation—without schemas, tables, APIs, or code.
This step ensures authority is predictable, auditable, and revocable.

🧩 CORE DEFINITIONS (AUTHORITATIVE)
Role

A Role is a named authority profile that groups permissions for a specific context.

Roles do not contain business logic

Roles do not imply identity

Roles are tenant-scoped unless explicitly governed otherwise

Permission

A Permission is an explicit allowance to perform a specific class of action on a defined scope.

Permissions are atomic

Permissions are explicit, never implied

Absence of permission = denial

Delegation

Delegation is the temporary granting of a subset of authority from one actor to another.

Delegation is explicit

Delegation is scoped

Delegation is revocable

🔐 ROLE SEMANTICS (INVARIANTS)

Roles exist only within a tenant context

A user may hold multiple roles

Roles do not grant authority outside their scope

Role assignment is governed and auditable

Role ambiguity → STOP

🔐 PERMISSION SEMANTICS (INVARIANTS)

Permissions define what can be done, not how

Permissions are evaluated at action time

Permissions never bypass governance or boundaries

Permissions do not cross tenant boundaries

Implicit permissions are forbidden

🔐 DELEGATION SEMANTICS (INVARIANTS)

Delegation must be explicitly granted

Delegation scope must be narrower than the grantor’s authority

Delegation has a defined duration or condition

Delegation is non-transitive by default

Delegation cannot override invariants

Delegation ambiguity → STOP

🚫 EXPLICITLY FORBIDDEN PATTERNS

❌ “Admin means everything”

❌ Implicit privilege escalation

❌ Permanent delegation without governance

❌ Cross-tenant role inheritance

❌ AI-held roles or permissions

🛑 STOP CONDITIONS (CRITICAL)

Immediate STOP is required if:

A role implies unstated permissions

A permission bypasses a boundary

Delegation scope exceeds grantor authority

Authority lineage cannot be determined

PHASE 26 — STEP 4
Authentication vs Authorization Semantics (Strict Separation)

Purpose:
Formally separate identity proof (who are you?) from permission to act (what are you allowed to do?).
This prevents security bugs caused by conflating login success with authority.

🔑 AUTHENTICATION — SEMANTICS

Definition:
Authentication is the process of proving identity.

What authentication establishes:

The actor’s identity is verified

The actor belongs to a known category (human, service, system)

The actor is associated with a tenant context (if applicable)

What authentication does NOT establish:

❌ Permission to perform actions

❌ Authority level

❌ Role membership

❌ Trust beyond identity proof

Invariant:

Successful authentication never implies authorization

🛂 AUTHORIZATION — SEMANTICS

Definition:
Authorization is the process of deciding whether an authenticated actor may perform a specific action.

What authorization establishes:

Whether an action is allowed at this moment

Based on:

Roles

Permissions

Delegation

Trust boundaries

Governance rules

What authorization does NOT establish:

❌ Identity proof

❌ Authentication validity

❌ Long-term trust

Invariant:

Authorization is evaluated per action, not per session

🧱 SEPARATION RULES (NON-NEGOTIABLE)

Authentication must occur before authorization

Authorization must be evaluated after authentication

Authentication success without authorization → DENY

Authorization without authentication → INVALID

Cached authorization decisions must respect context and time

🚨 FAILURE & AMBIGUITY HANDLING
Authentication Failure

Identity not proven

Result: STOP / DENY

No authorization attempt is made

Authorization Failure

Identity known, action not allowed

Result: DENY

Event is auditable

Ambiguity (Either Stage)

Identity unclear OR permission unclear

Result: STOP

Governance review required

🔐 SECURITY INVARIANTS

No action executes without both authentication and authorization

No fallback from authorization failure

No implicit trust from prior actions

No “admin shortcut” semantics

No AI bypass of auth checks
