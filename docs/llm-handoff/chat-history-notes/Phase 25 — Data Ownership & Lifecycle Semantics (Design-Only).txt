PHASE 25 — STEP 1
Data Ownership Taxonomy (Who Owns What)

Purpose:
Define clear categories of data and their owners, independent of storage, schemas, or implementation.
Ownership determines authority, control, retention rights, and deletion semantics.

🧭 DATA OWNERSHIP CATEGORIES (AUTHORITATIVE)
1️⃣ Tenant-Owned Data

Definition:
Data created by or on behalf of a tenant as part of their normal use of the system.

Includes:

Content and content versions

Tenant-specific configuration

User accounts within the tenant

Tenant metadata

Ownership Rules:

Owned exclusively by the tenant

Governed by tenant context and system governance

Never shared across tenants by default

Implications:

Tenant controls lifecycle within governance limits

Deletion and retention must respect tenant intent and legal constraints

2️⃣ System-Owned Data

Definition:
Data required for the operation, integrity, and governance of the platform itself.

Includes:

Governance rules and records

Phase logs and audit metadata

Platform configuration

System health and integrity data

Ownership Rules:

Owned by the system (not tenants)

Cannot be deleted by tenants

Retained per governance and compliance requirements

Implications:

Exists to protect system correctness and auditability

Supersedes tenant preferences when required

3️⃣ Derived Data

Definition:
Data generated by the system from other data.

Includes:

Rendered outputs

Aggregations

Indexes

Caches

Ownership Rules:

Ownership follows the source data

Has no independent authority or permanence

Implications:

Can be regenerated

Deletion of source data invalidates derived data

4️⃣ Transient / Ephemeral Data

Definition:
Short-lived data that exists only to support in-process operations.

Includes:

Session data

Temporary computations

Request-scoped state

Ownership Rules:

Owned by the system

No persistence guarantees

Implications:

Must not be relied upon for correctness

Automatically discarded

5️⃣ Audit & Governance Data

Definition:
Data created to ensure traceability, compliance, and accountability.

Includes:

Audit logs

Change histories

Governance decisions

Authorization records

Ownership Rules:

Owned by the system

Immutable once written

Retained per governance policy

Implications:

Cannot be altered or deleted to “clean history”

Required for legal and operational trust

6️⃣ AI-Related Data

Definition:
Data used by or produced through AI-assisted processes.

Includes:

AI prompts and suggestions

AI-generated drafts

AI decision traces (if any)

Ownership Rules:

Ownership follows source context (tenant or system)

AI does not own data

No implicit reuse or training without authorization

Implications:

Strong privacy and compliance constraints

Explicit governance required for reuse

PHASE 25 — STEP 2
Data Lifecycle Stages (Conceptual, Non-Technical)

Purpose:
Define how data moves over time—from creation to deletion—independent of schemas, storage engines, or code.
These stages establish what transitions are allowed, who can trigger them, and what must never happen.

🔄 CANONICAL DATA LIFECYCLE (AUTHORITATIVE)
1️⃣ Creation

Meaning:
The moment data first comes into existence in the system.

Rules:

Creation always occurs within a governed context

Data is immediately classified into an ownership category (Phase 25 — Step 1)

Unclassified data is invalid

Implication:
No “anonymous” or ownerless data can exist.

2️⃣ Mutation

Meaning:
Any authorized change to existing data.

Rules:

Only mutable data may be mutated

Mutation requires explicit authorization

All mutations are auditable

Implication:
History is preserved; silent overwrites are forbidden.

3️⃣ Publication / Activation

Meaning:
Data becomes active, visible, or usable outside its authoring context.

Rules:

Publication is a state transition, not duplication

Publication never transfers ownership

Publication never grants mutation rights

Implication:
Runtime access is controlled and read-only.

4️⃣ Retention

Meaning:
The period during which data must be preserved.

Rules:

Retention policies are governance-defined

Retention applies even if a tenant requests deletion

Retention duration may differ by data category

Implication:
Compliance overrides convenience.

5️⃣ Archival

Meaning:
Data is no longer active but is preserved for reference, compliance, or audit.

Rules:

Archived data is read-only

Archived data is excluded from normal operations

Archival does not imply deletion

Implication:
History is preserved without operational risk.

6️⃣ Deletion

Meaning:
The controlled removal of data from active systems.

Rules:

Deletion is explicit and governed

Deletion respects retention and legal holds

Deletion semantics differ by data category

Implication:
Deletion is a process, not a single action.

7️⃣ Expiry (Automatic)

Meaning:
System-triggered lifecycle transition based on policy or time.

Rules:

Applies only to eligible data categories

Must be deterministic and auditable

Never bypasses governance constraints

Implication:
Automation does not bypass accountability.

🔐 LIFECYCLE TRANSITION RULES (GLOBAL)

Lifecycle stages are monotonic (no time travel)

Transitions are explicit, not implicit

Illegal transitions trigger STOP

Derived and transient data inherit lifecycle from source

PHASE 25 — STEP 3
Data Immutability & Deletion Semantics (Non-Negotiable)

Purpose:
Define what data may change, what must never change, and what “deletion” truly means—independent of storage, schemas, or tooling.
These rules are constitutional and override convenience.

🔐 DATA IMMUTABILITY RULES (AUTHORITATIVE)
1️⃣ Immutable by Definition

The following data is immutable once written:

Audit & governance records

Phase history and decisions

Authorization and access logs

Compliance-relevant events

Rules:

No updates, edits, or overwrites

Corrections are recorded as new entries, never mutations

Immutability is permanent

Reason:
Trust, traceability, and legal defensibility.

2️⃣ Conditionally Mutable Data

The following data may be mutable under governance:

Tenant-owned content (pre-publication)

Configuration (via governed change)

User profile data (within tenant context)

Rules:

Mutations require explicit authorization

All changes are versioned and auditable

Mutation never destroys history

Reason:
Operational flexibility without loss of accountability.

3️⃣ Effectively Immutable States

Some data becomes effectively immutable after a transition:

Published content (runtime-visible)

Archived records

Closed phase records

Rules:

No mutation allowed after transition

Changes require creation of a new version/state

Rollbacks are not overwrites

Reason:
Protects runtime safety and historical truth.

🗑️ DELETION SEMANTICS (AUTHORITATIVE)
4️⃣ What Deletion IS

Deletion is a governed lifecycle transition, not a destructive shortcut.

Deletion may mean:

Removal from active operations

Removal from user access

Marking as deleted while retaining audit trace

Eventual physical removal only when legally allowed

5️⃣ What Deletion IS NOT

Deletion does NOT mean:

Erasing history or audit logs

Bypassing retention policies

Immediate physical destruction by default

Retroactively changing the past

6️⃣ Soft vs Hard Deletion (Conceptual)

Soft Deletion:

Data is no longer active

Metadata and audit trail remain

Reversible only if governance allows

Hard Deletion:

Physical removal of data

Allowed only after:

Retention period ends

No legal or compliance holds exist

Governance authorization

7️⃣ Tenant Deletion Requests

Rules:

Tenant requests do not override retention or law

System enforces minimum retention

Governance decides final deletion timing

Implication:
“Right to delete” is respected within legal bounds, not absolutely.

8️⃣ Derived & Transient Data Deletion

Rules:

Derived data is deleted when source data is deleted

Transient data is automatically discarded

No retention guarantees for transient data

🛑 STOP CONDITIONS (CRITICAL)

Immediate STOP is required if:

Immutable data is attempted to be changed

Deletion bypasses retention or audit

Deletion semantics are ambiguous

Physical destruction is attempted without authorization
